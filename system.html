Tonight, automatic

  1. 1The scheduler wakes the orchestrator.
  2. 2The orchestrator hands jobs to the workers.
  3. 3Workers read the approved news feeds.
  4. 4AI services help verify, rewrite, voice and draw.
  5. 5Self check every line, then sort by risk.
  6. 6Save the story with its sources and evidence.
  7. 7Saving a story sends a parent alert.
  8. 8The parent hears: tonight’s story is ready.

At bedtime, with people

  1. AThe parent opens the story, the facts and the sources.
  2. BThe parent approves, skips or asks for a simpler version.
  3. COnly approved stories appear on the kid’s shelf.
  4. DPip reads it aloud, and they talk about it together.

The two paths meet at the story store. Everything before it is automatic; nothing after it reaches a kid without the risk rules and, when needed, the parent.

Components use generic names on purpose. Today’s prototype runs as static pages: the orchestrator plays a labeled replay of one real agent run, stories and family rules live in the page, and Pip speaks with the browser’s built in voice. In the full service, each agent worker becomes a real AI call with its own tools and checks, and nothing reaches a kid without passing the risk rules and, when needed, the parent.
